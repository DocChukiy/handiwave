# Handiwave Nigeria Market Validation Playbook

Research date: 1 October 2026

## Executive decision

Handiwave should not launch as another nationwide directory that says "verified artisans and secure payments." Several Nigerian competitors already make nearly identical promises.

Handiwave should launch as a **managed, neighbourhood-dense service marketplace** with one clear promise:

> Tell us what is wrong, receive a clear itemised quote from a genuinely checked professional, pay through a protected process, and approve the job only after evidence that it was completed.

The product should initially win through operational reliability, not feature breadth:

- One launch area the team can physically support.
- Three to five high-frequency services.
- Ten to twenty carefully selected professionals.
- Assisted booking through the website and WhatsApp.
- Real response-time, arrival, completion, dispute, and payout standards.

## What Nigerian users are telling us

### Market signals supported by research

- Trust is a central adoption barrier. Nigerian digital-finance research repeatedly identifies fraud exposure, hidden charges, failed transactions, and low trust as barriers.
- Price sensitivity is high. Unexpected charges damage confidence, so labour, transport, inspection, materials, platform fees, and payment fees must be visible before payment.
- Digital payments are growing but are not universal. EFInA reported that 45% of Nigerian adults used digital financial services in 2023, while 40% made digital payments for goods and services.
- A browser-first approach is correct. NBS reported broad phone access but much lower internet access, while GSMA reported a large mobile-internet usage gap and limited smartphone ownership.
- WhatsApp and social channels are business infrastructure, not just advertising channels. GSMA found that 65% of surveyed Nigerian mobile-internet users had used social media for business.
- The supply problem is larger than discovery. Informal workers also need market access, business support, digital skills, recognised competence, reliable payment, and tools.
- Nigerian competitors already advertise identity checks, escrow or protected payments, ratings, nearby matching, WhatsApp booking, guarantees, insurance, referrals, and artisan finance.

### Qualitative complaints worth validating directly

Public Nigerian discussions repeatedly mention:

- Inflated material prices and vague lump-sum quotes.
- Poor workmanship and jobs that must be redone.
- Workers collecting money and abandoning jobs.
- Customers refusing to pay inspection charges after diagnosis.
- Customers wasting artisans' time with unfunded enquiries.
- Difficulty judging whether a worker's claimed workshop, experience, or certification is genuine.
- Distrust of the platform itself: users ask who verifies the verifier.

These are directional signals, not substitutes for interviews with Handiwave's intended users.

## If I were a Nigerian customer

### What would make me try Handiwave

1. **Real local availability:** I can see professionals who genuinely work in my neighbourhood today or tomorrow.
2. **Exact verification meaning:** The badge tells me whether identity, phone, address, skill, references, and completed Handiwave jobs were checked.
3. **Clear quote breakdown:** I see inspection, labour, transport, materials, platform charges, and payment charges separately.
4. **Proof before release:** Before-and-after photos, completion notes, and my confirmation are attached to the job.
5. **A person to call:** A visible Nigerian phone and WhatsApp support line handles lateness, no-shows, safety concerns, and disputes.
6. **A workmanship remedy:** A clearly limited correction window for eligible work is more believable than a vague "100% satisfaction" claim.
7. **Real reviews only:** Reviews come from completed, paid jobs and show the service, area, date, punctuality, and outcome.
8. **No forced app installation:** I can start from a link or WhatsApp and use the lightweight website.
9. **Fast certainty:** I know when someone will respond, when they will arrive, and what happens if they fail to appear.
10. **Honest early-stage presentation:** A small real network is more trustworthy than thousands of demo professionals.

### What would stop me using it

- Fake-looking names, ratings, reviews, job counts, or "2K+ verified" claims.
- A "verified" badge with no explanation.
- Being asked to pay the full amount before scope and materials are agreed.
- No local support number or clear dispute timeline.
- Hidden fees at checkout.
- Profiles that appear active but do not respond.
- A nationwide claim with weak supply in my area.
- Calling money "escrow" without explaining who legally holds it and under what terms.

## If I were a Nigerian artisan

### What would make me join and remain active

1. **Funded, serious jobs:** I should know the customer has paid an inspection fee or funded the approved job before I travel.
2. **No lead-selling:** Do not charge me to view phone numbers or compete for enquiries that may never become work.
3. **No joining fee:** Earn commission only after I successfully complete and get paid for a job.
4. **Fast, predictable payout:** Show the exact release condition, platform deduction, payment fee, available balance, and payout date.
5. **Fair cancellation rules:** If I travel or inspect and the customer cancels, I receive the stated inspection or transport amount.
6. **Easy quoting:** Let me send voice notes, photos, and an itemised quote without writing a formal proposal.
7. **Reputation that earns more:** Completed jobs, repeat clients, punctuality, response speed, and workmanship should improve my ranking.
8. **Control:** I choose my service radius, working hours, emergency availability, minimum job value, and whether to accept a request.
9. **Fair disputes:** Chat, photos, quote changes, arrival evidence, and completion evidence protect both sides.
10. **Assisted onboarding:** A real person can help me build my profile, photograph past work, and understand payment rules.

### What would make me leave

- Paying for leads that do not convert.
- Slow withdrawal or unclear deductions.
- Customers changing the scope after arrival.
- Unpaid diagnosis, transport, or material sourcing.
- Automatic penalties without evidence or appeal.
- Too many professionals competing for too few real jobs.
- Complicated forms, high data use, or English-only instructions.

## Handiwave's current strengths

The current product already includes much of the transactional foundation:

- Customer and artisan roles.
- Service discovery and professional profiles.
- Booking, availability, rescheduling, and job statuses.
- In-app messaging and notifications.
- Quote acceptance and Paystack checkout.
- Protected-payment, wallet, withdrawal, and commission records.
- Customer completion confirmation.
- Reviews limited to completed jobs.
- Disputes and admin tools.
- Portfolio-style reels.
- Responsive website and installable PWA.

This means Handiwave does not need more screens before validation. It needs real supply, clear trust rules, assisted operations, and honest proof.

## Product gaps to fix before public recruitment

### Priority 0: credibility

- Replace demo professionals, ratings, jobs, and prices with real data or clearly label the experience as a preview.
- Remove unsupported claims such as "2K+ verified professionals," "24/7," and unsupported city coverage.
- Change the launch message to a truthful area and service promise.
- Add a visible WhatsApp and telephone support route.
- Publish transparent fees, cancellation rules, dispute steps, refund rules, and payout timing.
- Obtain Nigerian legal advice before presenting the current payment arrangement as regulated "escrow." Until confirmed, use "protected payment" and explain the process plainly.

### Priority 1: trust and job clarity

- Add verification levels: `Identity checked`, `Skill checked`, and `Job-proven`.
- Store evidence and admin approval for each badge; never infer verification from profile completion.
- Add itemised quote lines for diagnosis, labour, transport, materials, and extras.
- Require customer approval whenever job scope or price changes.
- Add before-and-after job photos and completion notes.
- Add arrival confirmation, preferably a customer OTP or check-in action.
- Add an inspection-fee option and cancellation compensation.
- Add a limited correction-window policy for eligible work.

### Priority 2: low-friction access

- Add "Book through WhatsApp" with a prefilled service and area message.
- Support voice notes and compressed images in job chat.
- Keep pages lightweight and preserve partially completed forms.
- Send essential updates through both in-product notifications and WhatsApp/SMS during the pilot.
- Allow an operator to create a booking for a customer or artisan with recorded consent.

### Do not build yet

- Loans, insurance, savings, subscriptions, nationwide dispatch, AI assistants, complex loyalty tiers, or dozens of categories.
- These features add operational and regulatory risk before the core job-completion loop is proven.

## Recommended launch wedge

### Geography

Choose one compact launch cell where the founder or operations lead can intervene in person. Do not select a city based only on market size.

If launching in Lagos, use one connected cluster rather than "all Lagos," for example:

- Yaba–Surulere–Ikeja, or
- Lekki–Ajah estate clusters.

The correct cluster is the one with the strongest existing relationships with estates, artisans, hardware stores, shortlets, or property managers.

### Services

Start with three urgent or repeatable services:

- Plumbing.
- Electrical repair.
- AC, generator, or inverter repair based on local supply.

Add cleaning only if a reliable team and repeat-contract opportunity already exist.

Avoid categories such as events, beauty, education, health, auto, and business services during the first pilot. Their workflows, risks, pricing, and quality standards differ too much.

### Best first customer segment

Prioritise people who repeatedly need maintenance rather than one-off app shoppers:

1. Estate and facility managers.
2. Shortlet and small hotel operators.
3. Landlords and property managers.
4. Busy households and new movers within the launch cell.
5. Diaspora property owners only after on-ground reporting and support are proven.

Property operators offer concentrated demand, repeated jobs, clearer feedback, and lower acquisition cost than broad consumer advertising.

## Positioning to test

### Customer version

> Reliable home repairs without the guessing. Get a checked professional, an itemised quote, protected payment, and real support if anything goes wrong.

### Artisan version

> Real jobs near you, funded before you start. Quote clearly, build your reputation, and receive your money without chasing customers.

### What makes Handiwave different

Do not lead with "marketplace," "verified," or "escrow." Competitors already do.

Lead with observable service standards:

- Response within a stated window.
- Itemised quote before approval.
- Evidence for every completed job.
- Human support on both sides.
- Correction or resolution process with a deadline.
- Payout within a stated period after customer approval.

## Audience acquisition strategy

### 1. Founder-led concierge pilot

The first customers should not arrive through broad ads. Recruit them personally, take their requests through WhatsApp, and use Handiwave behind the scenes to complete the workflow.

This reveals the real job language, objections, pricing disputes, no-show causes, and support workload before scale.

### 2. Demand partnerships

Approach:

- Estate associations and resident WhatsApp administrators.
- Facility and property managers.
- Shortlet hosts and small hotels.
- Letting agents helping new tenants move in.
- Coworking spaces, small offices, schools, and clinics for maintenance needs.
- Hardware and electrical shops that receive customer requests for installers.

Offer a simple pilot: one support number, vetted local professionals, itemised quotes, job records, and a first-response standard.

### 3. Artisan supply channels

Recruit through:

- Referrals from respected master artisans.
- Trade associations and cooperatives.
- Hardware, plumbing, electrical, AC, generator, and solar-parts shops.
- ITF/SUPA and recognised training or certification networks.
- Estate maintenance teams.
- Existing professionals with visible proof of work and repeat customers.

Do not bulk-onboard hundreds. Recruit enough professionals to fulfil expected demand reliably in each service-area cell.

### 4. WhatsApp distribution

- Create one business support number with clear operating hours.
- Give estate managers and partners a short booking message and QR code.
- Use opt-in broadcast lists for service reminders and updates.
- Publish before-and-after proof, not generic graphics.
- Give every founding customer and professional a trackable referral code.
- Let users begin with one natural-language message: "I need a plumber in Yaba tomorrow."

### 5. Local search

- Create truthful service-area pages such as "Plumber in Yaba" only where active supply exists.
- Set up a verified Google Business Profile for the operating company.
- Publish practical local pricing and diagnostic guides.
- Ask real customers for Google and Handiwave reviews after successful jobs.
- Track calls, WhatsApp starts, requests, quotes, and completed jobs by page.

### 6. Proof-based social content

Use TikTok, Instagram, Facebook, and YouTube Shorts for:

- Before-and-after jobs.
- "What this repair should include" explainers.
- Itemised quote examples.
- Artisan introductions and tool demonstrations.
- Customer-approved completion stories.
- Common scam and safety warnings.

Do not use invented testimonials, job counters, or staged claims presented as real.

### 7. Referrals after service quality works

Test a simple two-sided referral after ten successful jobs:

- Customer receives a credit only after the referred person's first completed job.
- Artisan receives a bonus only after the referred professional completes a quality-approved job.
- Partners can receive a small share of Handiwave's platform revenue, not the artisan's labour amount.

Avoid a large ambassador programme before unit economics and fraud controls are understood.

### 8. Paid advertising last

Run small area-specific ads only after the pilot proves that requests become completed jobs. Optimise for completed paid bookings, not clicks, installations, signups, or WhatsApp messages.

## Two-week validation sprint

### Week 1: prove the problem and prepare supply

#### Day 1: choose the launch cell

- Select one compact service area.
- Select three services.
- Name one person responsible for every live booking.
- Define support hours and escalation rules.

#### Days 1–3: customer discovery

Interview at least 15 potential customers, including five property or facility operators.

Ask about the last real service job, not what they imagine they might do:

1. What broke, and how urgent was it?
2. How did you find the professional?
3. What made you trust or distrust them?
4. How was the price agreed and changed?
5. Who bought materials, and how were prices checked?
6. What went wrong or nearly went wrong?
7. How and when did you pay?
8. Would you have paid an inspection fee? How much felt fair?
9. What evidence would justify paying before final completion?
10. Will you give us your next real job to handle?

The final question is the strongest signal. Compliments are not validation.

#### Days 2–4: artisan discovery

Interview at least 15 professionals and inspect proof of previous work.

Ask:

1. Where did your last ten jobs come from?
2. How many enquiries became paid jobs?
3. How much time and transport did you lose on failed enquiries?
4. How do you charge for inspection, labour, materials, and transport?
5. What causes customers not to pay or to dispute work?
6. When do you need material money?
7. What platform fee would feel fair on a real completed job?
8. How quickly must payout arrive?
9. Would you use WhatsApp, the web app, or both?
10. Will you accept one real pilot job under Handiwave's evidence and payment rules?

#### Days 4–5: prepare the pilot

- Select 10–15 professionals; activate only those who pass checks and a practical or reference assessment.
- Record the meaning of every verification badge.
- Create itemised quote templates for the three services.
- Define inspection, cancellation, completion, correction, dispute, and payout rules.
- Replace unsupported homepage claims with launch-cell facts.
- Add WhatsApp-assisted booking and support contact.

### Week 2: run real jobs manually

#### Days 6–12

- Acquire 10 real service requests through direct outreach and partners.
- Manually match no more than three appropriate professionals per request.
- Observe quote time, customer questions, cancellations, travel, arrival, scope changes, completion, payment, and support.
- Call both sides after every job.
- Ask successful customers for one referral and permission to publish authentic proof.

#### Days 13–14

- Review every failed or delayed request.
- Fix only the repeated bottlenecks.
- Publish real service-area availability and proof.
- Decide whether to continue, narrow, change pricing, or pause.

## Pilot scorecard

Track a single north-star metric:

> Successfully completed, customer-confirmed, protected jobs.

Supporting measures:

- Qualified customer interviews completed.
- Qualified professionals interviewed, approved, and active.
- Request-to-first-response time.
- Request-to-quote conversion.
- Quote-to-funded-booking conversion.
- Arrival within agreed window.
- First-visit completion rate.
- Scope or price changes after acceptance.
- Cancellation and no-show rate by side.
- Dispute, refund, and correction rate.
- Time from customer approval to artisan payout.
- Customer referral rate based on actual referrals.
- Professional weekly retention.
- Contribution margin after payment fees, support, refunds, and incentives.

### Two-week continuation threshold

Continue investing if the pilot achieves approximately:

- 10 genuine service requests.
- At least 5 paid or funded jobs.
- At least 80% arrival within the agreed window.
- At least 80% completion without a second professional replacing the first.
- Every approved professional receives clear and timely settlement.
- At least 3 customers introduce another real prospect or request a second job.
- Both sides understand the protected-payment process without founder explanation every time.

These are learning thresholds, not permanent service-level targets.

## Pricing hypotheses to test

Do not lock pricing before interviews and real jobs.

Test these principles:

- Free customer and professional registration.
- No fee for professionals merely to receive or inspect available requests.
- A visible inspection fee where diagnosis or travel is required.
- A platform fee charged only on completed paid work.
- No hidden checkout amount.
- A pilot discount funded by Handiwave, not deducted unexpectedly from artisan earnings.

Paystack currently lists Nigerian local transaction pricing as 1.5% plus NGN 100, with the NGN 100 waived below NGN 2,500 and local fees capped at NGN 2,000. Transfer and applicable statutory charges must also be included in unit economics.

## Compliance and operational foundations

Before taking public volume:

- Register and verify the operating business and Paystack account.
- Obtain legal advice on protected payments, fund holding, refunds, commissions, and the use of the term "escrow."
- Use a licensed payment provider and documented settlement flow.
- Publish plain-language terms, privacy notice, cancellation policy, dispute policy, fee table, correction policy, and verification definitions.
- Assess NDPC registration obligations and implement consent, retention, access control, deletion, and breach procedures.
- Never display full NIN, BVN, IDs, home addresses, or bank details to users or unnecessary staff.
- Separate customer location access from public profile location.
- Establish incident handling for safety, fraud, property damage, harassment, and emergencies.
- Keep an audit trail for profile approvals, quotes, changes, payments, messages, completion evidence, refunds, and disputes.

## Immediate product decision

The correct next move is not a native app, a national launch, or paid mass advertising.

The correct next move is a **two-week, website-and-WhatsApp concierge pilot** in one area with real professionals and real jobs. Handiwave should use the existing product as the job record and payment workflow while humans actively manage trust and service quality.
